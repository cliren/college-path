Med + Business playbook

Care + systems. Passion + a life you can keep.

You can love medicine and want better operations, smarter tools, and sane hours. This page maps clinic ops, health tech, biotech translation, and public health/policy — without the “sell out or burn out” binary.

TL;DR

Helping people includes redesigning the waiting room, the referral loop, and the policy — not only the prescription.
  • Clinical skill and systems skill stack. One doesn’t cancel the other.
  • Money is data, not destiny. Use it to avoid traps — not as your personality.
  • Pick problems you’d still care about in 10 years (access, quality, trust, equity, workflow).
  • If you’re also on a physician/PA track, keep the premed playbook open in another tab.

Five lanes (you can remix)

1. Clinic operations / practice management

  • Scheduling, staffing, revenue cycle, patient flow, quality metrics.
  • Undergrad: ops research-ish thinking, Excel/SQL, psych of teams, healthcare admin intros.
  • Early roles: medical assistant → ops coordinator; scribe with eyes on workflow; clinic internship.

2. Health tech / clinical informatics

  • EHRs that don’t ruin mornings. Decision support. Telehealth. Data pipelines clinicians trust.
  • Skills: product sense, human factors, basic coding or analytics, privacy/ethics literacy.
  • Combine with clinical exposure so you don’t ship vibes into a busy ED.

3. Biotech / translation

  • Moving discoveries toward patients: trials ops, regulatory literacy, BD, lab-to-clinic storytelling.
  • Science depth helps; communication and project management often decide who ships.

4. Public health + policy

  • Population outcomes, financing, coverage, community programs, evaluation.
  • MPH / policy / econ / sociology pathways; field work > slogan essays.

5. Hybrid clinician-builder

  • MD/DO/PA/NP + MBA/MPH/informatics later — or ops/product first, clinical later.
  • Order is personal. Burnout from forcing a prestige sequence is still burnout.

What “good” looks like (signals, not flex)

  • Proximity to real workflows — shadowing that includes front desk and billing, not only the white coat moment.
  • One measurable improvement — reduced no-shows, clearer after-visit summaries, cleaner handoffs.
  • Ethics over growth hacks — patient trust is the product.
  • Writing that sounds human — case notes, one-pagers, and READMEs beat buzzword soup.

Pay targets (ops + clinical lanes)

Targets help you practice hard. They don’t replace caring about the work.
  • Clinic / health services managers: US median ~$141k; CA median ~$141k (top quartile much higher with seniority).
  • PA / NP: strong CA premiums vs US (often mid-$160ks).
  • Physician specialties: still set the high end of the map — see the salaries page for US + CA estimates.
  • Health tech / product pay tracks company stage more than BLS clinical tables — shadow before you romanticize.

Sustainable life framing

A career that only works if you never sleep is a bad product design — including when the product is you.
  • Build recoverable weeks: hard blocks + protected rest, not endless hero mode.
  • Choose mentors who have a life outside the spreadsheet.
  • Pay attention to role models’ constraints (debt, visas, caregiving) — not only their titles.
  • Passion is fuel. Systems (habits, teams, tools) are the engine.

Undergrad → early career starter map

  1. Take one course each in: microecon or org behavior, stats, and a bio/chem enough to talk to clinicians.
  2. Get 50–100 hours near a clinic/public-health site — ask how work actually moves.
  3. Ship a tiny project: dashboard, intake form redesign proposal, literature brief for a nonprofit.
  4. Learn one tool deeply (SQL, Figma, Python notebooks, or spreadsheet modeling) — depth beats app zoo.
  5. If med school is also on the table, protect GPA and see premed playbook + MCAT practice.
